Using Daniel's Diaries with an NDIS plan

Applies to: Daniel's Diaries, operated by Foundational Minds

The short version

Daniel's Diaries is a structured psychoeducational program that helps children build skills like emotional regulation, flexible thinking, resilience and social skills. Many families use it alongside the work they are already doing with a psychologist, occupational therapist, behaviour support practitioner or other allied health professional.

Daniel's Diaries is a mainstream, low-cost product. Whether a subscription can be paid from an NDIS plan depends on each participant's plan, goals, and how the plan is managed. Many self-managed and plan-managed participants purchase low-cost supports of this kind where they relate to the participant's disability support needs — especially when a treating practitioner has recommended the program as part of the child's support strategies.

Important: No product is "NDIS approved". Claimability is always a decision for the participant, their plan manager and the NDIA based on the participant's plan. If you are unsure, ask your plan manager or support coordinator before purchasing.

How it usually works for families

  1. Your practitioner recommends Daniel's Diaries as a between-session practice tool that supports the goals in your child's support plan, and gives you a private invite link.
  2. You create the family account and choose a plan. The subscription belongs to your family, so your child's access and progress never depend on a particular practitioner or funding arrangement.
  3. If your plan is self-managed or plan-managed, you can ask your plan manager whether the subscription can be claimed. A recommendation letter from your practitioner (they can generate one from their Practitioner Hub) is usually the key piece of evidence, together with your payment receipts.
  4. If your plan is NDIA (agency) managed, purchases are generally limited to registered providers, so the subscription would usually be paid privately.

For practitioners

The Practitioner Hub includes a recommendation letter generator: open a client, go to the Support Plan tab and switch to "NDIS recommendation letter". It pre-fills the child's goals, the developmental areas they target, and the program areas that count toward them, alongside your details and the subscription cost — ready to print or save as PDF for the family's plan manager.

Because the family holds the subscription, there is no conflict-of-interest question about you purchasing supports on a participant's behalf — your practitioner plan covers your professional tools only.

What Daniel's Diaries is (and isn't)

Daniel's Diaries is an educational wellbeing tool informed by evidence-based principles. It is not therapy, clinical treatment, or a substitute for professional advice, and it does not replace the services of your child's practitioner. Progress data in the app reflects engagement and self-report only.
