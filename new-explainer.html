How Daniel’s Diaries Creates Psychologically Sound Modules

For psychologists and clinical content reviewers

Daniel’s Diaries uses a structured, theory-driven system to ensure every learning module is developmentally appropriate, ethically sound, and consistent with psychological science — while removing the burden of writing or managing AI prompts.

The Core Workflow

1. Blueprint The psychologist defines the psychological intent using structured fields.
2. Generator AI generates content using approved theory, rules, and safeguards.
3. Review Content is reviewed and approved before release.
4. Publish Approved modules are released to children and parents.

Step 1: The Theory Library (Created Once)

Instead of repeatedly typing theory names or explanations, Daniel’s Diaries uses a shared Theory Library. Each theory is defined once, reviewed, and reused safely.

Each theory includes:

  • Theory name (e.g. Neuroplasticity, Cognitive Reframing)
  • Key mechanisms (1–3 core principles)
  • Age translations (how this theory is explained to different age groups)
  • Do / Don’t guidance (common misunderstandings or misuses)
  • Contraindications & sensitivity notes (trauma, anxiety, ASD/ADHD)
  • Allowed claims (to prevent medical or over-promising language)
  • Suggested activity types (reflection, role-play, behavioural practice)
  • Suggested outcome measures (simple scales, check-ins, observations)
This ensures that the AI never “invents” psychological theory or applies it incorrectly. All content is grounded in approved definitions.

Step 2: The Module Blueprint (Fast & Focused)

For each weekly module, psychologists complete a short Module Blueprint. This focuses only on professional judgment — not technical prompting.

Required inputs:

  • Cycle & week number
  • Age range
  • Super skill and sub-skill
  • Theory selection (from the library)
  • Learning objective (one clear sentence)
  • Skill target (what the child can do afterwards)
  • Metaphor or analogy (e.g. Brain Town roads)
  • Progression note (how this builds on previous weeks)
  • Interaction type (selected from a list)
  • Outcome measure (selected from a list)

Optional inputs:

  • Example scenarios
  • Neurodivergent-affirming or trauma-aware notes
Psychologists never need to write AI prompts. The system generates them automatically using a consistent, clinically-reviewed template.

Why Prompts Are Auto-Generated

Free-text prompts lead to inconsistent quality, unclear claims, and difficult clinical review.

By generating prompts automatically from structured data:

  • Every module follows the same psychological rules
  • Language stays developmentally appropriate
  • Claims are constrained to what is ethically acceptable
  • Content is easier to review, audit, and update

Clinical Oversight & Safety

Before publication, modules can be reviewed against:

  • The theory used
  • The intended learning outcome
  • Age-appropriateness
  • Language safety and tone
  • Neurodivergent-affirming principles

This ensures Daniel’s Diaries remains an educational, supportive tool — not a diagnostic or therapeutic replacement.
